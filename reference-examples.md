## endless-jimmy — band tradition, instrumental-only, pre-1972 era

**Target:** A certain late-1960s single-coil-through-cranked-tube-amp electric guitar tradition. The real artist is never named.

**Shape of the tradition:**
- Three-piece band (guitar / bass / drums).
- Analog recording era (reel-to-reel tape, 4-track mixing).
- Pedal-based tone architecture (germanium fuzz, rocker-controlled treble filter, rotating-speaker emulation, octave-up fuzz, amp spring reverb).
- Instrumental only — the real tradition had vocals but the skill forbids them to avoid lyric reproduction.
- Minor-pentatonic / Mixolydian note language.
- Jazz-trained rhythm section with conversational drumming and melodic counter-line bass.
- Playing techniques: thumb-over bass fretting, unison bends, behind-the-nut pulls, double-stop slides, controlled sustained feedback as a note, tremolo-bar dive-bombs.

**Register dial:**
- **CATHEDRAL** — introspective ballad, rotating-speaker cleans, spring reverb, controlled feedback as note, tender power, 60–85 BPM.
- **STORM** — loud blues-jam, germanium fuzz, octave-up, tremolo-bar dive-bombs, joyful aggression, 100–130 BPM.
- **BLEND** — 6 CATHEDRAL / 4 STORM across ten tracks.

**Album arc:**
1. Opener (free-time guitar intro).
2. The single (mid-tempo minor-key groove).
3. First ballad (CATHEDRAL).
4. Centerpiece jam (STORM, 7+ minutes).
5. The strange one (backwards tape, wide swirl).
6. Side B opener (bass-led groove).
7. Standard twelve-bar minor blues.
8. The heaviest (STORM, confrontational).
9. Second ballad (CATHEDRAL, emotional summit).
10. Closer (feedback into tape hiss).

**Phantom-artist naming grammar:**
- Band name: leader-plus-band / poetic collective / neutral three-word.
- No project aliases (band tradition, not a composer with brackets).
- Album titles: cosmic/weather/sleep or blues-adjacent/road, 2–4 words.
- Track titles: place-and-weather / person-and-address / motion-journey / abstract-cosmic / 12-bar-blues-shaped / outro-shaped.
- Phantom label: single evocative word or word + suffix.
- Catalogue number prefix: 3 letters derived from label name.
- Release year: 1969–1974.

**Prose prompt target:** 180–320 words.

**When to use this as structural reference:**
- Target is a specific band or small ensemble (3–6 members).
- Analog or pre-digital production era.
- Pedal/effect-chain-based tone architecture.
- Guitar / bass / drums + optional keys instrumentation.
- Instrumental-only or textural-only vocal policy.
- Blues, rock, folk, soul, jazz-rock, or similar band traditions.

## endless-hiro — composer tradition, textural vocals, 2010s era

**Target:** A certain 2010s Tokyo cinematic hybrid-orchestra composer. The real artist is never named, nor his project aliases, collaborators, franchises, or tracks.

**Shape of the tradition:**
- Hybrid ensemble: four-tier stack of orchestra + rock band + choir+soprano + electronic layer.
- Modern cinematic production (hyper-wide stereo, sub-bass to 30 Hz, bright extended high end to 15 kHz, close-mic'd rock + concert-hall orchestra + cathedral choir).
- Textural vocals only — pseudo-Latin, wordless, abstract non-semantic fragments. No coherent narrative lyrics.
- Minor-key dominant with modal mixture for lift chords.
- Motivic economy: small 3–5 note kernels developed by reharmonization, register shift, mode change.
- Crescendo architecture: solo opening → tier-stacking → climax → release, often with two peaks.
- Stylized project alias for vocal collaborations (brackets, slashes, phonetic transliteration, numeric stylizations).

**Register dial:**
- **VIGIL** — piano-and-strings-and-soprano lamenting, cathedral reverb on choir, minimal rock band, 60–85 BPM, beautiful melancholy.
- **CHARGE** — full-stack action, sixteenth-note string ostinatos, four-on-the-floor kick with sub-bass, French horn fanfares, choir in parallel octaves, electric guitar power chords, 120–160 BPM, heroic defiance.
- **BLEND** — 5 VIGIL / 5 CHARGE across ten tracks.

**Album arc:**
1. Main theme opener (piano-first that climbs to a full-stack hit).
2. The battle cue (CHARGE, 142 BPM, soprano + choir).
3. The piano meditation (VIGIL, piano throughout).
4. The vocal piece (soprano feature over orchestra + choir).
5. The ostinato engine (CHARGE, dotted-eighth from bar one).
6. The lament (VIGIL, solo violin, no drums).
7. The rock-orchestra hybrid (CHARGE, distorted guitar + brass + double-kick).
8. The reprise variation (main theme returned bigger, mode shift).
9. The two-peak centerpiece (VIGIL → CHARGE → VIGIL → bigger CHARGE).
10. The piano denouement (VIGIL, main theme reduced to kernel).

**Phantom-artist naming grammar:**
- Composer name: Japanese full name or stylized mononymous — never a real composer.
- Project alias: brackets like `[rvl]`, slashes like `/vox:∞/`, phonetic like `[/əˈlaɪəs/]`, symbol stylizations. Never `[nZk]` or variants.
- Album titles: single evocative English / short Latin or German phrases / stylized hybrids with brackets, numeric suffixes.
- Track titles: phonetic IPA transliterations / bracket-slash stylizations / German or Latin phrases / abstract English / compound-stylized hybrids.
- Phantom label: single evocative English or Latin word, often with suffix (Division, Sound, Records, Lab).
- Catalogue number prefix: 3–4 letters derived from label name.
- Release year: 2010–2024.
- Optional: phantom OST framing + phantom vocalist with stage name and style tic.

**Prose prompt target:** 200–340 words (the four-tier stack needs more words).

**When to use this as structural reference:**
- Target is a composer or producer with named project (not a band).
- Modern digital or hybrid production (post-2008).
- Large ensemble with multiple co-equal tiers.
- Textural-vocals policy.
- Project-alias stylizations are part of the aesthetic.
- Film score, trailer music, cinematic hybrid, modern orchestral, hybrid-orchestra traditions.

## Choosing between them

| If your target is... | Use as structural reference |
|---|---|
| A rock band or small ensemble (3–6 pieces) | endless-jimmy |
| A composer with scored works | endless-hiro |
| A solo electronic producer | endless-hiro (adapt tiers to synth/drums/processing/vocals) |
| A DJ or mix-tradition artist | endless-jimmy (adapt to single-mix form) or hybrid |
| A jazz quartet or chamber group | endless-jimmy (adapt to acoustic instrumentation) |
| A genre or scene (not a specific artist) | Whichever matches the typical ensemble size and era |
| A classical composer | Consider the `endless-overtures` real-composer variant instead |
| A singer-songwriter | endless-jimmy structure, but revisit vocal policy carefully |
| A rapper or MC | endless-jimmy adapted to track-list arc, vocal policy as lyrical-permitted |
| A film composer | endless-hiro |

Read the SKILL.md and references of whichever matches more closely before authoring your own. The folder structure is identical; the content differs.

## When neither reference quite fits

If the target's structural shape doesn't match either canonical example — a solo folk singer with voice-and-guitar, a jazz big band, a drum-and-bass producer, an orchestra-only composer, an a cappella vocal ensemble — adapt the closer of the two and flag the divergences in a short note at the top of the generated SKILL.md. The chain still works; the content differs more.

Common adaptations:
- **Solo artist, no band**: use endless-jimmy structure, make instrumentation one-sided, drop the three-voice conversation framing.
- **Big band / large ensemble**: use endless-hiro's tier model but rename the tiers to match (e.g. reeds / brass / rhythm / soloists for jazz big band).
- **Pure electronic producer**: use endless-hiro's tier model, replace orchestra with synth-architecture tiers.
- **Vocal-centric tradition**: use endless-hiro structure with lyrical-permitted vocal policy and tighter rules on lyric content invention.
- **DJ / mix tradition**: use endless-jimmy structure but change album arc from song-list to mix-arc (intro / peak / breakdown / closer).
